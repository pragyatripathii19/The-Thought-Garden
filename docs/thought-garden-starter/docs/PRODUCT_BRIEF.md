# Product Brief: The Thought Garden

## 1. Vision

**Every thought has a home, even when you don't know what that home is yet.**

The Thought Garden helps people capture whatever is on their mind, find related thoughts again, and gradually develop ideas without requiring them to organize everything upfront.

## 2. The problem

People collect fragments across notebooks, notes apps, drafts, screenshots, and random documents. Many thoughts are unfinished or difficult to categorize at the moment of capture. Traditional systems often expect people to maintain folders, tags, or elaborate structures. Over time, potentially useful ideas become a pile that is hard to revisit.

The product hypothesis is that **low-friction capture plus useful reconnection over time** is more valuable to this audience than another system for manually organizing notes.

## 3. Initial audience

People who regularly have ideas, observations, questions, or personal reflections but struggle to keep them organized and rediscover them. The first validation cohort should include people who already complain about scattered notes or abandoned note-taking systems.

This is a hypothesis, not a validated market segment. Do not assume all users want the same level of automation.

## 4. Core job to be done

“When a thought comes to me, help me save it immediately without deciding where it belongs, then help me reconnect with it when it becomes relevant again.”

## 5. Product promise

- Capture without categorizing.
- Keep original thoughts intact.
- Surface plausible relationships between thoughts.
- Help ideas evolve without forcing them into tasks or projects.
- Make it easy to search, revisit, export, and keep ownership of personal data.

## 6. Differentiation hypothesis

Automatic categorization alone is not a strong differentiator. The product should focus on helping users **pick up the thread of an idea later**: explain why two thoughts may be related, let users confirm or reject the relationship, preserve context, and make older thoughts easy to rediscover.

## 7. Product principles

1. Capture first; organization can happen later.
2. Unfinished thoughts are first-class content.
3. Suggestions are optional, explainable, and reversible.
4. User-authored content is never silently rewritten or merged.
5. The interface stays calm and low-maintenance.
6. Local-first storage and clear export keep the user in control.

## 8. Success signals for the prototype

Observe whether test users can:

- Save a thought in a few seconds with no required metadata.
- Find an older thought using approximate wording or a related phrase.
- Understand and correctly judge a suggested connection.
- Return to the app and find a resurfaced thought useful rather than intrusive.
- Explain why they would use this alongside or instead of their current notes app.

Positive feedback is not enough. Look for repeat use and willingness to pay for the product as it exists, not for imagined future features.

## 9. Business assumptions to test

- Users experience enough pain to adopt a separate capture tool.
- Reconnection is valuable enough to distinguish the product from free notes apps.
- Users trust a local-first tool with personal thoughts.
- A small subset will pay for a focused, reliable experience.

## 10. Non-goals for the first prototype

No account system, cloud sync, team collaboration, social features, voice transcription, browser extension, mobile app, AI chat, automatic rewriting, nested folder system, complex mind map, or gamified streaks. These add cost and complexity before the core behaviour is validated.
